### Why This Structure?

**Asset Protection:**
- If CDLS gets sued → Patents owned by LLC, not CDLS (protected)
- If you get sued personally → Patents owned by LLC, not you (protected)
- If LLC gets sued → Trust owns LLC, creditors face charging order limitations

**Tax Efficiency:**
- LLC = disregarded entity (no separate tax return)
- Royalty income flows to you individually (1040 Schedule C)
- Trust = revocable (no gift tax, no estate tax during lifetime)

**Estate Planning:**
- Trust avoids probate (patents transfer immediately upon death)
- Successor trustee takes over (no court involvement)
- Privacy (trust not public record, unlike will)

**Operational Flexibility:**
- LLC signs contracts (not you personally)
- Professional credibility (LLC vs "Julio, Individual Inventor")
- Easy to add co-inventors later (issue LLC membership units)

#### Step 4: Create Revocable Living Trust (Day 4-5 - Thursday-Friday)

**What is a Revocable Living Trust?**

A legal arrangement where you (Grantor) transfer assets to a Trust, which is managed by you (Trustee) for your benefit (Beneficiary), with instructions for distribution upon death to your heirs (Successor Beneficiaries).

**Key Features:**
- **Revocable:** You can modify or cancel anytime
- **You're in Control:** You're the Trustee during lifetime
- **Avoids Probate:** Assets transfer immediately upon death
- **Privacy:** Not public record (unlike will)
- **Creditor Protection:** Limited (revocable trusts offer less protection than irrevocable)

**Two Options for Creating Trust:**

**OPTION A: DIY (Cheapest - $0-$50)**

**Recommended Software:**
- **Nolo's Quicken WillMaker & Trust:** $99 (one-time)
  - https://www.nolo.com/products/quicken-willmaker-plus
  - Step-by-step interview
  - Generates California-compliant trust
  - Includes pour-over will

- **LegalZoom Living Trust:** $279+
  - https://www.legalzoom.com/personal/estate-planning/living-trust-overview.html
  - More expensive but includes attorney review

**OPTION B: Attorney (More Expensive - $1,500-$3,000)**

**When to Use Attorney:**
- You have complex estate (multiple properties, businesses)
- You want tax planning advice (estate tax, gift tax)
- You have minor children (guardianship provisions)
- You have special needs beneficiaries

**For Your Situation:** DIY is sufficient (simple estate, no dependents yet, assets under $13M estate tax threshold)

### PHASE 4: TAX & REGULATORY COMPLIANCE (Week 2-3)

#### Step 9: File IRS Form 8832 (Entity Classification Election)

**What is Form 8832?**
Tells IRS to treat your LLC as a "disregarded entity" (not separate from you for tax purposes).

**Why File This?**
- Single-member LLC owned by revocable trust = disregarded entity by default
- Filing confirms this treatment (avoids IRS confusion)
- Royalty income flows to you individually (Schedule C on Form 1040)

**When to File:**
Within 75 days of LLC formation (by April 14, 2026 if formed Jan 29)

**How to File:**

**Download Form 8832:**
https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f8832.pdf

**Complete Form:**

**Line 1:** Julio Automotive Innovation LLC

**Line 2a (EIN):** [Your LLC's EIN from Step 2]

**Line 4 (Election):** Disregarded as an entity separate from its owner

**Line 5 (Effective Date):** January 29, 2026 (LLC formation date)

**Line 6 (Owner):**
The Julio [Last Name] Revocable Living Trust dated January 31, 2026
Trustee: Julio [Last Name]
SSN: [Your SSN - trust uses grantor's SSN]

**Sign and Date:**

**Mail to:**
```
Department of the Treasury
Internal Revenue Service Center
Ogden, UT 84201-0023
```

### Scenario 2: You Get Sued Personally

**Situation:** You're in a car accident. Victim sues you for $5M. Your insurance only covers $1M.

**Without Trust/LLC Structure:**
- Victim gets $4M judgment against you personally
- Your assets (including patents) can be seized to satisfy judgment
- You lose patent rights

**With Trust/LLC Structure:**
- Victim gets judgment against you
- Patents owned by LLC (not you personally)
- LLC owned by Trust (not you personally)
- Creditor limited to "charging order":
  - Can receive distributions FROM LLC if/when made
  - Cannot force sale of LLC
  - Cannot seize patents inside LLC
- You retain control of LLC and patents ✅

**Charging Order Limitation (California Law):**
California Revised Uniform Limited Liability Company Act § 17705.03:
> "On application to a court of competent jurisdiction by a judgment creditor of a member or transferee, the court may charge the membership interest of the judgment debtor with payment of the unsatisfied amount of the judgment... The court may order a foreclosure of the charging order..."

**BUT:** In single-member LLCs, courts have sometimes allowed foreclosure. **This is why the Trust owns the LLC** - creates an additional layer of protection.

### Q4: Do I need separate tax returns for LLC and Trust?

**A: No.**

**LLC:** Disregarded entity (IRS treats as if it doesn't exist)
- No separate tax return (Form 1065)
- Income/expenses flow to you personally
- Report on Schedule C (Form 1040)

**Trust:** Grantor trust (IRS treats as if it doesn't exist during your lifetime)
- No separate tax return (Form 1041) while you're alive
- Uses your SSN (not separate EIN)
- Income flows to you personally

**Only after you die:** Trust becomes separate taxpayer (files Form 1041).

---

### Q5: How do I pay myself from LLC?

**A: Three options:**

**Option 1: Owner's Draw (Recommended)**
- Transfer money from LLC bank account to personal account anytime
- No limit (you own 100% of LLC via Trust)
- Not taxable event (you already pay tax on LLC profits)
- Record in LLC books: "Distribution to Member"

**Option 2: Salary (Not Recommended for Single-Member LLC)**
- Would require payroll taxes (FICA, Medicare)
- More paperwork
- No benefit (LLC is disregarded entity anyway)

**Option 3: Leave in LLC**
- Accumulate funds for future patent filings, legal fees
- Still taxed personally (LLC profits flow through)
- Can distribute later when needed

**Best practice:** Take monthly/quarterly distributions to personal account. Leave enough in LLC for upcoming expenses (patent fees, attorney fees).
