1. CONSTITUTIONAL AUTHORITY
   Question: Can a ballot initiative mandate insurance purchase?
   Analysis: 
   - Federal: Post-NFIB v. Sebelius (ACA case), states CAN mandate
   - California: No constitutional barrier
   - Precedent: Auto insurance mandate upheld
   Conclusion: YES, mandate is permissible

2. SINGLE-SUBJECT RULE
   Question: Does Unified Shield violate single-subject requirement?
   Analysis:
   - California Constitution Art. II, Sec. 8(d)
   - Must be "reasonably germane" to single subject
   - Subject: "Universal insurance coverage for all Californians"
   - Health/auto/home components all relate to that subject
   Conclusion: LIKELY COMPLIES (but prepare for challenge)

3. APPROPRIATIONS LIMITATIONS
   Question: Can initiative appropriate $25B from General Fund?
   Analysis:
   - Art. XVI, Sec. 1 (Legislature controls appropriations)
   - Exception: Initiative can appropriate if part of comprehensive scheme
   - Better approach: Structure as self-funded (premium revenue)
   Conclusion: AVOID direct appropriation; use bond authority

4. TAKINGS CLAUSE  
   Question: Does mandate constitute unconstitutional taking?
   Analysis:
   - Not taking property, just requiring purchase
   - Similar to auto insurance, health insurance (ACA)
   - State has police power to regulate insurance
   Conclusion: NO takings violation

5. VESTED RIGHTS
   Question: Do existing insurers have vested rights to market?
   Analysis:
   - No right to conduct business free of regulation
   - Unified Shield doesn't prohibit private insurance
   - Supplemental market remains available
   Conclusion: NO vested rights violated

6. FEDERAL PREEMPTION
   Question: Does ERISA preempt state mandate?
   Analysis:
   - ERISA governs employer-sponsored plans
   - Unified Shield applies to individuals, not employer plans
   - Coordination provisions needed
   Conclusion: CAN coexist with ERISA if properly structured

OVERALL CONCLUSION:
Ballot initiative is LEGALLY VIABLE with careful drafting.
Primary risk: Single-subject challenge (prepare for litigation).
