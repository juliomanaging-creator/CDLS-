PART 1: THE PROBLEM (2 minutes)
========================================================================

"Let me start with the problem we're solving.

California dealers currently pay $1.20 to $2.50 per mile - about $400 per vehicle on average - to third-party brokers for vehicle transportation. That's auction-to-dealer, dealer-to-dealer transfers, customer deliveries - all the logistics that keep dealerships running.

Here's the critical insight: with 1,200+ CNCDA member dealerships transporting 2.5 to 3 million vehicles annually, dealers are paying over $1 billion per year to brokers while maintaining zero ownership stake in the infrastructure they depend on every single day.

This creates three major problems:
• First, premium pricing - brokers add 15-25% margins with no value returned to dealers
• Second, capacity constraints - during peak seasons, dealers can't get trucks when they need them most
• Third, administrative burden - every dealer separately manages CARB compliance, Clean Truck Check, carrier vetting

What if dealers owned their logistics infrastructure instead? That's exactly what we're building."

PART 2: THE SOLUTION (3 minutes)
========================================================================

"The Syndicate is California's first dealer-owned logistics cooperative. We're applying a proven vertical integration model - the same strategy Carvana used to cut logistics costs from $400 to $200 per vehicle - to California's organized dealer network.

Here's how it works operationally:

Phase 1 - Infrastructure: We establish 3-4 regional hubs along the I-5 corridor - Sacramento, Bay Area, LA, San Diego. We acquire an initial fleet of 15-20 company-owned car haulers for high-density routes, and we deploy a professional Transportation Management System with real-time GPS tracking and route optimization.

Phase 2 - Scale: We grow from 30,000 vehicles in Year 1 to 100,000 by Year 3, expanding our fleet and optimizing routes based on actual demand patterns.

Phase 3 - Returns: By Year 5, we're projecting $37.5M in revenue, $8.6M in net income, and 25% return on equity for limited partners.

The economics are compelling:
• Dealers save 30-50% versus current broker costs - that's $160 per vehicle on average
• Limited partners earn 18-24% IRR through equity ownership
• California gains 150-200 jobs and retains $120-240M in annual savings in-state

This isn't speculative. Carvana and CarMax already proved these economics work at scale. We're just giving California dealers access to the same advantages."

Competitive Risk - 'What if brokers cut prices?'

Regulatory Risk - 'What about California's zero-emission requirements?'
