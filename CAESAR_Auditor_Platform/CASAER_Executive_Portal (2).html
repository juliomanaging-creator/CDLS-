The Problem CASAER Solves

California is losing billions to fraud that current staff cannot investigate

The State Auditor's office receives 2,636 fraud allegations annually but can only fully investigate 72 of them — a 2.7% throughput rate. EDD alone lost $513M to confirmed fraud in 2024, plus $1.5B in improper payments. The GAO estimates the federal government loses $233B–$521B annually to fraud. This is not a performance failure — it is a structural capacity crisis.

Workforce Expansion Justification

The Core Argument

CASAER doesn't replace auditors — it generates more cases than current staff can investigate. With 100,000 transactions analyzed in under 3 seconds, the bottleneck shifts from "we can't find the fraud" to "we found it but don't have the people to investigate." Federal grant applications that pair technology with workforce expansion score dramatically higher than technology-only proposals.

The Multiplier Effect

Every dollar invested in audit capacity returns $8–$16 in identified fraud, waste, and improper payments (GAO). Hiring 25–40 CASAER-trained auditors at $3.1M–$5.2M annually could generate $25M–$80M+ in recoveries — a 5x–16x return before grant funding is considered.

Three Pathways to Competitive Compensation

Pathway 1: CalHR Special Salary Adjustment (SSA)
CalHR authorizes SSAs when classification pay is not competitive. CASAER defines a new benchmarkable skill set (R-based forensic analytics). BU 12 MOU already includes 4% annual adjustments — SSA is additive. Fastest path.
Pathway 2: New Classification — "Data Analytics Auditor"
New CalHR classification with R statistical computing, ML-assisted analysis, and Benford's forensic detection. Separate pay scale starting at $105K–$140K. Does not disrupt existing classifications. Strongest long-term solution.
Pathway 3: Grant-Funded Salary Supplementation
Federal grants routinely fund "competitive compensation" as workforce development. SNAP grants funded technology AND personnel at market rates. Signals seriousness to grant reviewers about retention.

The Retention Math

Current turnover cost: ~20% vacancy × 110 staff = ~22 departures/yr × $50K–$75K replacement = $1.1M–$1.65M wasted annually

Cost of 15–20% raise: $1.3M–$2.2M total

Break-even: Raise pays for itself if turnover drops by just 50%. Also improves investigation throughput, institutional knowledge, and grant compliance capacity.
