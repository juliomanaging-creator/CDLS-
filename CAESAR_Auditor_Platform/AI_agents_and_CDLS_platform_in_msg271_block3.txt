DESIGN: Copper in LEAVES/STEMS, not ROOTS/FRUITS

Protocol:
1. Expose plant to copper AFTER root development (Week 3+)
2. Copper transports preferentially to LEAVES (photosynthesis sites)
3. Roots remain relatively LOW copper (<50 ppm, safe range!)
4. Fruits/seeds develop late (after copper exposure stops)

Result:
- Leaves: 1,000 ppm copper (conductive, not eaten by most animals)
- Stems: 500 ppm copper (conductive, woody/unpalatable)
- Roots: 20-50 ppm copper (SAFE for consumption!) ✅
- Seeds/fruits: <10 ppm copper (SAFE, nutritious!) ✅

Wildlife impact:
- Herbivores avoid leaves (taste bitter, naturally deterred)
- Seed-eaters eat safe seeds (birds, rodents)
- Root-eaters (gophers, etc.) consume safe roots
- Plant provides food WHILE being functional! 🌱🦌✅
