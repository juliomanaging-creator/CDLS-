Similar to supercapacitors, but using plant tissues:

NORMAL SUPERCAPACITOR:
- Electrode: Activated carbon (high surface area)
- Electrolyte: Aqueous solution (ions for charge storage)
- Separator: Porous membrane
- Capacitance: 100-300 F/g

PLANT ROOT "SUPERCAPACITOR":
- Electrode: Conductive polymer-infused root tissue
- Electrolyte: Plant sap (water + ions - K+, Ca²+, Mg²+)
- Separator: Cell walls (natural porous membrane!)
- Capacitance: 50-150 F/g (lower than commercial, but functional!)

Charge storage mechanism:
1. Apply voltage across roots (cathode and anode connections)
2. Ions in sap migrate to opposite charges
3. Positive ions (K+, Ca²+) accumulate at cathode
4. Negative ions (Cl⁻, NO3⁻) accumulate at anode
5. Creates electric field (stores energy!)

Discharge:
- Remove voltage source
- Ions return to equilibrium
- Energy released as current

Capacity calculation (example):
- Root mass: 100 grams (typical small plant)
- Capacitance: 100 F/g (optimistic, with conductive polymers)
- Total capacitance: 10,000 F (10 kF)
- Voltage: 1.2 V (safe for plant, doesn't cause electrolysis)
- Energy stored: E = ½ CV² = ½ × 10,000 × (1.2)² = 7,200 Joules (2 Wh)

Result: Small but FUNCTIONAL energy storage! ✅
